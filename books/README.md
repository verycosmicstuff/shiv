# Complete Works of Shiv Kumar Batalvi (ਸਿ਼ਵ ਕੁਮਾਰ ਬਟਾਲਵੀ)

### 1936 – 1973 • Sultan of Birha (ਸੁਲਤਾਨ-ਏ-ਬਿਰਹਾ / سلطانِ برہا)

> *"Birha, birha aakhiye, birha toon sultan..."*
> *"Separation, they speak of separation—yet thou alone, O Separation, art the Sovereign."*

---

## 📖 Corpus Architecture & Overview

This directory houses the complete archival markdown repository of Shiv Kumar Batalvi's poetry, compiled book by book with rigorous scholarly fidelity. Every poem is preserved in four parallel linguistic registers:

1. **ਗੁਰਮੁਖੀ (Gurmukhi)**: The authentic Punjabi script in which Shiv composed his verses.
2. **شاہ مکھی (Shahmukhi)**: The Perso-Arabic Punjabi script widely read across Western Punjab (Pakistan).
3. **Roman Transliteration**: Phonetic pronunciation guide preserving lyrical cadence, metre, and rhyme.
4. **English Poetic Translation**: Faithful literary rendition rendering subtle pastoral idioms and Sufi metaphors.
5. **Scholarly Apparatus**: Stanza-by-stanza literary commentary, regional cultural glossaries, and historical citations linked to verified archival memoirs.

## 🏛️ Academic Bibliography & Primary Sources

- **`[GARGI-1979-SURME]` Balwant Gargi (1979)**: *Surme Wali Akh (The Kohl-Lined Eye)*. Navyug Publishers, Delhi (pp. 142–168). [Primary Memoir]
  > *Balwant Gargi documents Shiv’s chaotic writing habits: composing complete poems on Scissors and Gold Flake cigarette foil packets, café napkins in the Indian Coffee House Chandigarh, and tavern chits, frequently leaving drafts on tables or giving them away to casual admirers.*

- **`[AMRITA-1974-NAGMANI]` Amrita Pritam (1973)**: *Nagmani (Special Memorial Edition on Shiv Kumar)*. Nagmani Press, New Delhi (Vol. 85, June 1973). [Primary Memoir]
  > *Amrita Pritam writes: "Death did not steal Shiv; Shiv courted death as his final bride." She catalogs his uncollected verses and reflects on the intellectual hostility he faced from dogmatic critics.*

- **`[SEKHON-1972-PUNJABI-KAVI]` Sant Singh Sekhon (1972)**: *Punjabi Kavi Ate Kavitriyan (Marxist Critical Essays)*. Lahore Book Shop, Ludhiana (pp. 89–112). [Literary Criticism]
  > *Sant Singh Sekhon termed Shiv the "Keats of Punjabi poetry" for his meter and folk metaphor, but charged him with "palayanwad" (reactionary escapism), arguing that romanticizing youth-suicide was reactionary in post-Partition agrarian Punjab.*

- **`[PAASH-1973-SIARH]` Avtar Singh Sandhu (Paash) (1973)**: *Siarh (Polemical Essays of the Naxalite Poetry Movement)*. Siarh Collective, Jalandhar (Autumn Issue 1973). [Literary Criticism]
  > *Paash contrasted the armed peasant rebellion with Shiv’s vulnerable sorrow: "We do not need weeping songs for dying youth; we need weapons against feudal landlords." Yet Paash admitted Shiv’s raw emotional authenticity was unmatched.*

- **`[SAHITYA-1967-LOONA]` Sahitya Akademi Secretariat (1967)**: *Sahitya Akademi Awards Official Citation: Loona (1965)*. Sahitya Akademi, Rabindra Bhavan, New Delhi (Citation Registry 1967). [Institutional Record]
  > *Awarded to Shiv Kumar Batalvi for his epic blank-verse play Loona. At age 31, he became the youngest recipient in the history of the National Academy of Letters, sparking envy and moral controversy.*

- **`[SHARMA-1979-SOLITARY]` Dr. O.P. Sharma (1979)**: *Shiv Batalvi: A Solitary and Passionate Singer*. Sterling Publishers, New Delhi (pp. 12–45, 180–205). [Primary Memoir]
  > *Authoritative academic biography documenting his childhood in Bara Pind Lohtian (now Pakistan), the trauma of the 1947 Partition flight, his clerkship at the State Bank of India Chandigarh, and his final collapse in Kir Mangyal.*

- **`[AIR-ARCHIVES-JALANDHAR]` Akashvani Archives Committee (1975)**: *All India Radio (AIR Jalandhar & Delhi) Station Retention Protocols*. Directorate General, All India Radio, New Delhi (Spool Audit Report 1971–1975). [Institutional Record]
  > *Historical administrative audit revealing that chronic shortages of 2-inch quadruplex magnetic video and 1/4-inch audio spools mandated routine bulk-erasure and re-use of tapes for daily bulletins, inadvertently destroying dozens of Batalvi’s live mushaira recordings.*

- **`[BBC-1972-KAUL]` Mahendra Kaul (Producer) (1972)**: *BBC TV London: "Nai Zindagi Naya Jeevan" – Shiv Kumar Batalvi Interview*. BBC Television / British Film Institute National Archive (Broadcast August 1972 (BFI Item 44892)). [Archival Broadcast]
  > *Shiv responds to critics on BBC television: "People ask why I write only of tears and death. A poet is not an ideological contractor. Pain has its own sanctity; human longing does not need a political party slogan to justify its tears."*

- **`[BATALVI-ARUNA-1988]` Aruna Batalvi (1988)**: *Oral History Archive: Punjabi University Patiala*. Department of Punjabi Literary Studies, Punjabi University (Tape Interview Series 4, Side B). [Primary Memoir]
  > *Shiv’s wife Aruna confirms that following his death, his bachelor accommodations in Sector 21 Chandigarh were vacated in haste; landlords and municipal cleaners incinerated boxes containing handwritten diaries, unpublished notebooks, and correspondence.*

- **`[LAHORE-BOOKSHOP-1974]` Mohan Bhandari & Jiwan Singh (Eds.) (1974)**: *Shiv Kumar: Sampooran Kaav Sangreh (Complete Poetical Works)*. Lahore Book Shop, Ludhiana (Editor Foreword, pp. 5–18). [Literary Criticism]
  > *The standard definitive Punjabi anthology gathering 211 authenticated surviving lyric poems, songs, and ghazals across his nine lifetime and posthumous collections, plus Loona and Main Te Main.*

- **`[QADIR-YAR-1840]` Qadir Yar (1840)**: *Qissa Puran Bhagat (Classical Punjabi Epic)*. Historical Manuscript / Lahore Editions (Verses 140–210). [Literary Criticism]
  > *The 19th-century patriarchal text that cast Loona as a lustful evil stepmother and Puran as a saint. Shiv radically overturned this narrative in 1965, exposing the feudal commodification of teenage girls by elderly monarchs.*

- **`[KISHAN-SINGH-1971]` Prof. Kishan Singh (1971)**: *Sahit Di Parakh (Evaluation of Literature)*. Lokgeet Prakashan, Sirhind (pp. 175–194). [Literary Criticism]
  > *Kishan Singh’s counter-Marxist appraisal: arguing that Shiv’s Birha was not decadent escapism, but a profound Punjabi ontological condition rooted in Sufi tradition (Shah Hussain, Baba Farid, Bulleh Shah).*

- **`[BRITISH-LIBRARY-1972]` Indian Workers’ Association UK (1972)**: *Tour Recordings of Shiv Kumar Batalvi in Southall & Birmingham*. British Library Asian & African Collections (Sound Archive) (Tape Shelfmark C128/90). [Archival Broadcast]
